RGB channel shift

X and Y move a channel in pixels; Z scales it about the centre, so a positive Z on one channel gives a radial fringe.

Luminance · Y

Chroma · Cb / Cr

Cb is blue minus luma, Cr is red minus luma, both centred on 128. Offsets move the whole image on the vectorscope; gain pushes it outward; rotate spins hue.

Advanced: the individual steps

Reads the photo's fingerprint from the parts you didn't touch and gives the edited parts the same one: softness, colour resolution, black level, highlight saturation, then the seam, then grain last. Everything is measured; the one slider is how far to go. Check the Seam map afterwards: baked looks evenly speckled, an unbaked edit shows as a bright island.

Regrain

—
seed 1

Reads the grain the image already carries in its flat, untouched areas (σ per luminance, grain size, chroma level), finds where local texture falls below that, and adds back only the missing variance, with the same size and luminance curve. Areas whose grain is intact get nothing, nor do clipped blacks and whites, so the result reads as one capture rather than a layer. The Grain map view shows where and how much is added.

Compression

Your edits are applied in the JPEG's own YCbCr space and written to the file without a round trip through RGB. 4:4:4 keeps chroma at full resolution; at quality 90 and up the quantisation steps are 1–3 levels, so Cb/Cr edits come back almost exactly. 4:2:0 averages every 2×2 chroma block and is what most platforms re-encode to.
